The program works on paper. Each level below prints as one packet; everything you need at the guitar is in it.

Print the packet

Open the level and use “Download PDF” (or Print, US Letter, headers and footers off). In order: the overview (goals, reading list, songs), the month week by week (week plan, when to switch songs, exit checklist, what to do if stuck or if it's too easy), the drills, the four session pages, chord shapes, scales, the song charts, the practice log and the check-in sheet. Print the four session pages once; you reuse them every week.

Four kinds of day

  • A · Chord day — new shapes and the week's three change pairs, with the full drill recipe on the hardest one.
  • B · Fretboard day — notes, scales, keys and numbers; the level's chords applied in a key.
  • C · Song day — the longest song block: isolate the hard change, then play the section and the song in time.
  • D · Mix and measure — short versions of everything, the benchmarks with numbers, one review item from the previous level, and five minutes of making your own progression.

Every session is exactly 45 minutes and touches chords, fretboard and a song. Each starts with a 3–5 minute primer on the day's shapes (no generic warm-up — do that in your own playing time) and ends with a 3-minute log.

The weekly rhythm

Four sessions a week, in any order as long as A comes before C (C puts A's new change into the song). One rhythm that works: A Monday · B Tuesday · C Thursday · D Saturday. Consecutive days are fine — new shapes settle overnight, so short and frequent beats long and rare.

The same four pages stay fresh for a month because of the week plan: when a block says “this week's pair” or “this week's section”, look up the row for the week you circled at the top of the page.

Using the log

  • On each session page: circle the week, write the date, tick each block, write the tempo or count in every blank.
  • On the log page: tick the session and write how many minutes it really took.
  • In every Session D: run the benchmarks cold and write the numbers in that week's column. The numbers climbing is the progress; a number that hasn't moved in two weeks means change the drill, not do more of it.

Moving between levels

  • Move on when every box on the exit checklist is ticked — usually after about four weeks, but the checklist decides.
  • Stuck after six weeks? Move on anyway. Keep one song from the level going in the next level's Session D, and start the next level's Session A primer with your weakest pair until it reaches its target.
  • Too easy? If you pass every benchmark cold in week 1's Session D, go straight to the next level.
  • Each level's Session D carries one review item from the level before, so nothing fades.

Bring back for a check-in

When you leave a level, fill in its check-in sheet (the last page of the packet) and bring it with the log page: final benchmark numbers, the chords and changes that still feel bad, the songs you loved or hated, how long sessions really took, and what you want more or less of. The next level is re-tuned from that sheet before you print it — Levels 3–6 are first drafts for exactly that reason.
