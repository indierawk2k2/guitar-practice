A practice program for a player who already strums open and barre chords in good time and wants a wider chord vocabulary, a map of the fretboard and enough theory to know what the fingers are doing. It runs in six levels, each built around real songs that force the new shapes in time.

Two halves

  • Read on the phone, away from the guitar. The Learn chapters explain how chords are built and named, keys and numbers, the fretboard, scales, and the mechanics of changing chords. Each level lists the chapters to read that month. The chord and scale libraries and the song charts are there when you want to look something up.
  • Practise from paper, at the music stand. Each level prints as one packet: four 45-minute session pages (A Chord day, B Fretboard day, C Song day, D Mix and measure), a week plan, the drills, large chord and scale diagrams, the song charts, a practice log and a check-in sheet. No screen needed at the guitar — a metronome is all.

A level is designed for about four weeks of four sessions, but you leave it when its exit checklist is ticked — earlier or later. Levels 3–6 are first drafts, to be re-tuned from your practice log before you print them.

Start here, today

  1. Print the Level 1 packet (Practice → Level 1, “Download PDF”). Read its first two pages.
  2. Read {{link:learn:changing-chords | chapter 8, Changing chords}} before the first session — it explains the drills the packet names. If you read only one chapter, read that one.
  3. Over the first two weeks, read chapters 1–8 in the order the packet lists.
  4. Play Session A. Circle week 1, write the date, follow the blocks, write the numbers.

Diagram key everywhere: chord boxes have the low E string on the left; fretboard diagrams have high E on top; a square is the root.
