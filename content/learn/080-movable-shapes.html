--- title: Movable shapes order: 80 summary: E-shape and A-shape barres in every quality, finding any chord from its root on string 6 or 5, and the partial versions that save your hand. level: 2 ---

You already play major barres on the E and A shapes. The same two hand positions give you every other chord quality too: each is a known open shape with the index finger playing the part of the nut. Learn a quality once, and you can play it in all twelve keys by sliding.

The one idea

An open E chord is a shape plus four open strings. Slide the shape up a fret and lay the index across where the nut was, and those open strings become barred strings. Every interval relationship stays the same, so the chord quality stays the same; only the root changes. Whatever you can do to an open E or A shape — lift the 3rd, drop the octave root to a ♭7 — you can do to its barre.

The square in each diagram is where the name comes from: string 6 for E-shapes, string 5 for A-shapes. You need the natural notes on those two strings by heart (Chapter 10 has the full map):

Sharps and flats sit between: B♭ is string 6 fret 6 or string 5 fret 1; F♯ is string 6 fret 2 or string 5 fret 9.

E-shape family (root on string 6)

Shown at G, fret 3. Slide the whole shape to change the root: fret 1 = F, fret 5 = A, fret 8 = C.

{{chords: G_barre_E3, Gm_barre_E3, G7_barre_E3, Gm7_barre_E3}}
  • Minor: lift the finger on string 3 (the 3rd). The barre now plays the ♭3 there.
  • 7: lift the finger on string 4 (the octave root). The barre plays the ♭7 there — open E7 moved up.
  • m7: do both. Index plus one finger: the easiest barre there is.
{{chords: Gmaj7_barre_E3, Gsus4_barre_E3, G7sus4_barre_E3, G6_barre_E3}}
  • maj7: a full-barre E-shape maj7 is awkward. The usable version mutes string 5 and plays root, 7, 3, 5 (3x443x).
  • sus4: string 3 moves up from the 3rd to the 4th, beside the others at fret 5.
  • 6: a four-string shape with string 5 muted; the 6th is on string 4.
{{chords: Gm6_barre_E3, Gm7b5_barre_E3, Gdim7_barre_E3, G13_barre_E3}}

The last row belongs to Chapter 14, but they're the same hand position and worth seeing now: m7♭5, dim7 and 13 all keep the root on string 6 and mute string 5.

A-shape family (root on string 5)

Shown at C, fret 3. Fret 1 = B♭, fret 2 = B, fret 5 = D, fret 7 = E.

{{chords: C_barre_A3, Cm_barre_A3, C7_barre_A3, Cm7_barre_A3}}
  • Major: the ring finger flattens across strings 4–2. If it can't avoid string 1, mute it.
  • Minor: Am moved up. The 3rd (string 2) is a fret lower, so the fingers spread out.
  • 7 and m7: string 3 (the octave root) is left to the barre, which plays the ♭7.
{{chords: Cmaj7_barre_A3, Csus2_barre_A3, Csus4_barre_A3, C6_barre_A3}}
  • maj7: string 3 drops one fret below the root — the 7.
  • sus2: string 2 drops to the barre (the 2nd). Easy, and it rings beautifully.
  • sus4: string 2 rises a fret (the 4th).
  • 6: the A6 shape: one finger flat across strings 4–1.
{{chords: Cm6_barre_A3, Cm7b5_barre_A3, Cdim7_barre_A3, C9_barre_A3}}

Economy versions

A full barre is not always the right tool. In a band, or under a fast strum, three or four strings often sound better and cost far less.

{{chords: G_part_E3, G_thumb_E3, Gm_part_E3, C_part_A3, Cm_part_A3, G5_barre_E3}}
  • Top four strings of the E-shape (xx5433) are the small F shape moved up. Bright, and only a two-string barre.
  • Thumb over: the thumb frets the root on string 6 and damps string 5. Common in folk, soul and indie playing; needs a reasonably slim neck.
  • A-shape without a barre: three separate fingers on fret 5 and string 1 muted (x3555x). Many players find this easier than the ring-finger barre.
  • Power chords: root and 5th only. When you only need the bass and weight, not the colour.

Choosing between E-shape and A-shape

Every chord is available in both shapes: G is an E-shape at fret 3 or an A-shape at fret 10; C is an A-shape at fret 3 or an E-shape at fret 8. Choose the one that keeps the hand near the previous chord. In a key, a pattern appears: with the I chord as an E-shape at fret n, the IV is an A-shape at the same fret and the V an A-shape two frets up.

Key of GIiiiiiIVVvi
ChordGAmBmCDEm
Shape, fretE-shape 3E-shape 5A-shape 2A-shape 3A-shape 5A-shape 7

Slide that whole map to fret 5 and you're in A; to fret 1, F; to fret 6, B♭. Any key, same hand positions. That's what “movable” buys you.

{{grid: G | Em | C | D ; title="I–vi–IV–V with barres: G (E3), Em (A7), C (A3), D (A5)"}} {{tip: The minor-shape stretch}}

The A-shape minor (x13421) asks finger 2 to sit a fret behind fingers 3 and 4, on the string next to them. If your hand resists, practise the shape with air shapes and place and lift (Chapter 8) before you try to change into it.

{{/tip}} {{try: What to take to the guitar}}
  • At fret 3, play G, Gm, G7, Gm7 as E-shapes, then C, Cm, C7, Cm7 as A-shapes. Say which finger you lifted and what note the barre now plays.
  • Play I–vi–IV–V in G with barres, then slide the map to A (fret 5) and B♭ (fret 6).
  • Play the same progression with economy versions only. Compare the sound.
{{/try}}

Check yourself

Where is the root of an E-shape barre, and of an A-shape?

E-shape: string 6, under the barre. A-shape: string 5, under the barre.

You need B♭m. Give one E-shape and one A-shape position.

E-shape minor at fret 6 (string 6, fret 6 = B♭). A-shape minor at fret 1 (string 5, fret 1 = B♭).

How does an E-shape major barre become a 7th chord?

Lift the finger on string 4. That string was the octave root; under the barre it plays the ♭7.

In the key of A with the I chord as an E-shape at fret 5, where are IV and V?

IV (D): A-shape at fret 5. V (E): A-shape at fret 7.
