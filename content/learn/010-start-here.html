You already play. You can hold down open chords and major barres, you keep time, and you can get through a song. What this site adds is vocabulary and fluency: more chord types, a clear idea of why they are called what they are called, the mechanics of getting between them cleanly, and a map of keys and scales across the whole neck.

Two halves

Practice is a program of six levels. Each level is one printable packet: an overview, four 45-minute sessions (A, B, C, D), reference pages with every chord and scale the level uses, song charts, and a practice log. Print it and put it on the music stand. You move on when every exit criterion is ticked, not when a calendar says so.

Learn is this reading. Each chapter is short, built for a phone, and meant to be read away from the guitar. The badge next to a chapter title (“Level 2”) is the level where that chapter starts to matter; read a little ahead of where you are playing.

  1. Colour the open chords — maj7, 7, m7, sus, add9, 6; the number system.
  2. Movable minors and sevenths — barre shapes in every quality, any key, borrowed chords.
  3. Triads and inversions — three-note shapes, slash chords, bass lines, CAGED.
  4. Tension chords — diminished, half-diminished, augmented, 9ths, 13ths, secondary dominants.
  5. Voice leading and modes — maj7/m7 vamps, Dorian, Mixolydian, Lydian.
  6. Beyond standard tuning, and shell voicings.

Chord boxes

A chord box is the neck standing upright, facing you.

  • Low E string on the left, high E on the right.
  • The thick bar at the top is the nut. If the shape sits higher up, the nut is replaced by a fret number on the left (“5fr” = the top row is fret 5).
  • x above a string: don't play it. o: play it open.
  • Numbers in the dots are fingers: 1 index, 2 middle, 3 ring, 4 little, T thumb.
  • A long rounded bar is a barre: one finger flat across several strings.
  • Square = root. Everywhere on this site the root is a square — a filled square on a fretted string, a hollow square above an open string, a small window in a barre.
  • Under the strings: the interval each string plays (R, 3, 5, ♭7 …). Chapters 2–4 explain those labels; once you can read them, a chord box tells you why the chord sounds the way it does.

Fretboard diagrams

Scales and note maps lie the neck on its side, the way tab does: high E on top, low E at the bottom, nut on the left, fret numbers underneath. Filled squares are roots; rings are the other notes. Inside each dot is either the interval (R, ♭3, 5 …) or the note name. Wide diagrams scroll sideways on a phone.

{{scale: A_minor_pent_pos1}}

Yes, the two diagram types put the low E in different places. Chord boxes show a guitar standing upright and facing you; neck diagrams copy tab. Both conventions are universal, so it's worth getting used to both.

Bar grids

Progressions are drawn as bars, four to a row. The slashes at the bottom of each bar are beats.

{{grid: G | Em | C . D . | G ; repeat=2}}
  • One chord in a bar: it lasts the whole bar.
  • Two chords: two beats each. C . D . means the same thing, written as four slots. C . . D would be three beats of C, one of D.
  • % repeats the previous bar. The heavy barlines with dots and “×2” mean play the section twice.

Roman numerals

Chord charts in the chapters often use numbers instead of letters: I, IV, V, vi. The numeral is the position of the chord's root in the key; the case tells you the quality.

Numbers let you see that two songs in different keys are doing the same thing, and they let you transpose without thinking. Chapter 6 covers them properly.

Check yourself

In a chord box, which side is the low E string?

The left. On fretboard (scale) diagrams it's the bottom line.

What does a square dot mean?

The root of the chord or scale. Open roots are hollow squares above the nut; roots under a barre are small square windows in the bar.

What does C . . G mean in a bar?

Three beats of C, then one beat of G.

In roman numerals, what's the difference between IV and iv?

Same root, different quality: IV is major, iv is minor. In G, IV is C and iv is Cm.
