Retuning even one string rewrites part of the map you've spent this whole site building. That's the point: shapes your hand doesn't know produce sounds your habits wouldn't, and open strings ring in new places. Plenty of guitarists you admire live here — Sonic Youth built much of their sound on unusual tunings — and a few tunings are common enough to know properly.

Every chord diagram in this chapter is drawn for its own tuning. Chord boxes don't print string names, so the tuning is in the section heading and on each chord's library card; fretboard diagrams label every string with its new note.

What retuning changes

Standard tuning is 4ths between strings, with a major 3rd between G and B (Chapter 2). Each alternate tuning changes one or more of those gaps, and every shape that crosses a changed gap shifts:

  • A string tuned down a whole step: every note on it is two frets higher than before. Shapes on that string move up two frets; shapes elsewhere are untouched.
  • The gap to its neighbour widens or narrows by the same amount, so interval shapes across that pair change.
  • Open strings are now different notes — new drones, new open chords.

Drop D (D A D G B E)

String 6 down a whole step to D. One change:

  • Strings 6 and 5 are now a 5th apart (D–A), and 6 and 4 an octave. So the bottom three strings fretted straight across at any fret are a power chord — one finger.
  • Anything rooted on string 6 moves up two frets: the G at fret 3 is now at fret 5.
  • Everything on strings 5–1 is unchanged, so all your A-shape and D-shape chords still work, with a big low D available underneath.
{{chords: D5_dropD, G5_dropD, D_dropD}}

Use it for heavy riffs (one-finger power chords) and for songs in D, where the low D can drone under everything. The Beatles' “Dear Prudence” is in drop D, fingerpicked over that low string.

To tune: lower string 6 until its 12th-fret harmonic matches the open D string, or until fret 7 matches the open A string.

Double drop D (D A D G B D)

Both E strings down to D. Now the top string is also D, so the B–D gap at the top is a minor 3rd instead of a 4th. Shapes on string 1 move up two frets: open D's F♯ is now at fret 4. Neil Young's “Cinnamon Girl” is the famous example. Open D-based chords ring with the root top and bottom.

{{chords: D_ddropD, G_ddropD}}

DADGAD (D A D G A D)

Strings 6, 2 and 1 each down a whole step. Played open, it's Dsus4 — D A D G A D, with no 3rd — so it's neither major nor minor until you fret something. That ambiguity is the whole appeal: drones, modal melodies, Celtic and folk fingerstyle, and Led Zeppelin's “Kashmir”.

{{chords: Dsus4_dadgad, D5_dadgad, D_dadgad, Gadd9_dadgad}} {{scale: D_major_dadgad_neck}}

What to notice: three D strings and two A strings mean roots and 5ths are everywhere. Fret a melody on strings 1–3 and let the rest ring as a drone; most of the chords in this tuning are a melody note over open D and A.

Open G (D G D G B D)

Strings 6, 5 and 1 down a whole step. Strummed open it's a G chord. Consequences:

  • A straight barre at any fret is a major chord: fret 5 is C, fret 7 is D. I – IV – V is open, 5, 7.
  • The root is on string 5 (and 3), not string 6 — the low D is the 5th. Keith Richards famously takes the low string off and plays open G on five strings.
  • The classic rhythm move is the IV-over-I shape: fret strings 4 and 2 (and 1) against the open G to get C/G, and rock between the two.
{{chords: G_openG, C_openG, C_over_G_openG}} {{scale: G_major_openG_neck}}

Open D (D A D F♯ A D)

Strings 6, 3, 2 and 1 changed: the G string drops a half step to F♯, the B and high E strings a whole step. Open, it's a D chord, root on the bottom. As in open G, a barre at fret 5 is G and at fret 7 is A. It's the slide-guitar tuning, and it suits drone-heavy songs in D.

Finding chords in any tuning

When you meet a tuning you've never used, don't hunt for shapes at random. Work it out the same way the decoder in Chapter 4 works:

  1. Write the tuning down and the interval between each pair of strings. Mark which pairs differ from standard.
  2. Find the open chord. What do the open strings spell? (Dsus4 for DADGAD, G for open G.) That's your home.
  3. Find the roots of the key on every string, nut to fret 7.
  4. Find the 3rds. For a major chord on a root, locate the major 3rd on a neighbouring string. For minor, a fret lower. These are the notes that decide everything; the rest can be roots and 5ths.
  5. Use one-finger barres and partial barres across strings that form a 5th or octave — they're the power chords of that tuning.
  6. Let open strings ring. In most alternate tunings the open strings are the key's 1 and 5. Any fretted note over them is a chord.
  7. Check by ear and by name. Spell what you're fretting string by string. If it spells a known chord, name it.
{{tip: Keep a page per tuning}}

When you learn a song in an alternate tuning, write the tuning and the shapes you found on one page, with the open-string notes at the top. Next time you visit that tuning you start from there instead of from zero.

{{/tip}} {{try: What to take to the guitar}}
  • Tune to drop D. Play a riff with one-finger power chords on strings 6-5-4, then play D – G – A using open-string drones.
  • Tune to DADGAD. Strum it open (Dsus4), then fret string 3 at fret 2 (D5), then add string 4 at fret 4 (D major). Hear the 3rd arrive.
  • Tune to open G. Play I – IV – V as open, fret 5, fret 7. Then rock between G and the C/G shape.
{{/try}}

Check yourself

In drop D, where is the G power chord with its root on string 6?

Fret 5, one finger across strings 6, 5 and 4 (G D G).

Which strings change in DADGAD, and what chord do the open strings make?

Strings 6, 2 and 1 go down a whole step (E→D, B→A, E→D). Open, it's Dsus4.

In open G, a barre at fret 5 gives which chord, and why?

C. Every open string is a note of G major; raising them all by five half steps makes every string a note of C major.

A string is tuned down a whole step. What happens to shapes that use it?

Every note on that string is now two frets higher, so the part of any shape on that string moves up two frets.
