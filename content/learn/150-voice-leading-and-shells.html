So far a chord change has meant a hand shape change. Voice leading is a different way of seeing it: each chord is several separate voices, like singers in a choir, and a good change is one where each voice moves as little as possible. It's what makes a progression sound smooth rather than blocky, and it's the reason sophisticated-sounding chord parts are often physically easier, not harder.

The smallest-movement principle

Going from one chord to the next:

  1. Keep any note the two chords share (the common tones) in the same voice.
  2. Move every other voice to the nearest note of the new chord — a half or whole step if possible.
  3. Let the bass jump if it has to; the upper voices shouldn't.

C → Am shares C and E, so only G has to move — up a whole step to A. C → F shares C; E rises a half step to F, G rises a whole step to A. On the top three strings that's tiny movement in one area of the neck, instead of a jump between two open shapes.

{{grid: C | Am/C | F/C | G/B ; title="Voice-led on strings 3-2-1"}}

Frets on strings 3-2-1: C at 5-5-3 (C E G), Am/C at 5-5-5 (C E A), F/C at 5-6-5 (C F A), G/B at 4-3-3 (B D G). One or two fingers move each time, and the hand never leaves frets 3–6. Compare that with open C, Am, F, G and listen to how much more connected the top line sounds.

Guide tones: the 3rd and the 7th

In a seventh chord, the root is often played by the bass and the 5th adds little. The two notes that define the chord's quality — major or minor, dominant or major 7 — are the 3rd and the 7th. They're called guide tones because a line through them guides the ear through the progression.

In a ii – V – I they move beautifully:

Chord3rd7th
Dm7FC
G7BF
Cmaj7EB

Follow them: Dm7's 7th (C) falls a half step to G7's 3rd (B). Dm7's 3rd (F) stays as G7's 7th. Then G7's 7th (F) falls to Cmaj7's 3rd (E), and G7's 3rd (B) stays as Cmaj7's 7th. The 7th of each chord falls a half step to the 3rd of the next, and the 3rd stays to become the next 7th. That's the engine of jazz harmony, and it works in any chain of chords a 5th apart.

Shell voicings

A shell is root, 3rd and 7th — nothing else. Three strings, very little effort, all the information. Two layouts cover everything:

  • Root on string 6, strings 6-4-3: root, 7th, 3rd (string 5 muted).
  • Root on string 5, strings 5-4-3: root, 3rd, 7th.
{{chords: Gmaj7_shell643_3, G7_shell643_3, Gm7_shell643_3}} {{chords: Cmaj7_shell543_3, C7_shell543_3, Cm7_shell543_3}}

Each set of three differs by one finger. From maj7 to 7, the 7th drops a fret. From 7 to m7, the 3rd drops a fret. That's the whole system.

Two more layouts swap the order — root, 3rd, 7th from string 6, and root, 7th, 3rd from string 5 (skipping string 4):

ii – V – I with shells

Alternate the 5th-string and 6th-string layouts and the guide tones line up by themselves:

  • Dm7: 5-4-3 shell at fret 5 (x535xx): D, F, C.
  • G7: 6-4-3 shell at fret 3 (3x34xx): G, F, B.
  • Cmaj7: 5-4-3 shell at fret 3 (x324xx): C, E, B.

Watch string 4: F, F, E. String 3: C, B, B. The hand barely moves; the harmony moves a lot.

{{tip: Shells in a band}}

Shells leave room for everyone. With a bass player covering the root, you can even drop the root and play just the 3rd and 7th on strings 4 and 3 — two notes that still say exactly which chord it is.

{{/tip}}

maj7 and m7 vamps, voice-led

Back to the vamps from Chapter 15: Amaj7 – Dmaj7, Am7 – D7. Play them as shells and find the version where the hand moves least. Open Amaj7 (x02120) to Dmaj7 (xx0222): the G♯ on string 3 moves up to A, the open top E moves up to F♯, and the C♯ on string 2 stays put — small steps, big colour change.

Chord melody basics

Chord melody means playing a tune and its harmony at once. The method:

  1. Melody on top. Find the melody on strings 1 and 2, around one position.
  2. Chord underneath. On each important melody note (usually beat 1 of the bar), play a triad or shell whose top note is that melody note. Triads on strings 1-2-3 (Chapter 13) are perfect: choose the inversion with the melody note on top.
  3. Single notes between. Passing melody notes are played alone; let the chord ring under them.
  4. Add a bass note on strings 5 or 6 when you can reach it. Shells with a melody note added on string 2 or 1 are the next step.

Start with a melody you can sing — a nursery tune or the vocal line of a song you know well — and harmonise only beat 1 of each bar. It's slow at first and it trains exactly the skill this chapter is about: knowing which note of the chord is on top, and where it goes next.

{{try: What to take to the guitar}}
  • Play the six shells (maj7, 7, m7 on both root strings), saying “7th drops” and “3rd drops” as you change.
  • Play Dm7 – G7 – Cmaj7 with shells, then move it to other keys by starting the first shell on a different fret.
  • Play only the 3rd and 7th of the same ii – V – I on strings 4 and 3.
  • Harmonise the first phrase of a melody you know with 1-2-3 triads, melody on top.
{{/try}}

Check yourself

What are the guide tones of G7, and why are they called that?

B (3rd) and F (♭7). They define the chord's quality, and a line through them guides the ear from chord to chord.

In a chain of chords a 5th apart, what happens to the 7th of each chord?

It falls a half step (or a whole step) to become the 3rd of the next chord.

How do you turn a maj7 shell into a 7 shell, and a 7 into an m7?

Lower the 7th a fret (maj7 → 7). Then lower the 3rd a fret (7 → m7).

What three notes make a shell voicing, and which note is usually dropped first from a full chord?

Root, 3rd and 7th. The 5th goes first.
